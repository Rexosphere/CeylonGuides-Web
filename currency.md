Trusted Banks & ATMs

    Major Banks: Commercial Bank, Sampath Bank, Hatton National Bank (HNB) and Bank of Ceylon (BOC) all have extensive ATM networks. Their ATMs (often on the LankaPay interbank network) accept Visa/Mastercard. They are generally safe and reliable.

    ATM Limits & Fees: Daily withdrawal limits vary (often ~₨40,000–100,000 per transaction). Many banks allow ₨100,000+ per day, but confirm your card’s limit. ATMs typically charge up to ₨1,000 per withdrawal; some machines (especially BOC) may be fee-free
    wise.com
    . Currency conversion fees (charged by your home bank) may also apply.

    Tips: Always use the ATM’s official bank network (look for LankaPay logos). Inform your bank of travel plans to avoid blockages. Use ATMs during banking hours if possible.

Tipping Etiquette

    Restaurants & Cafés: Check your bill – many mid-to-upscale places automatically add a 10% service charge. If no charge is included, a tip of 5–10% of the bill is customary
    themiddleagewanderer.com
    . At small local eateries, a few hundred rupees (≈₨100–150) is appreciated. Leave tips in cash.

    Drivers & Guides: For taxis and tuk-tuks, you can round up the fare or add about ₨ 100–200 per ride. For long drives, ~10% of the fare is good if service was polite
    themiddleagewanderer.com
    . For private day tours, tipping ₨ 500–1,000 (US$5–10) per person per half-day is common
    themiddleagewanderer.com
    . Larger tips may be given for exceptional tours or safari guides (₨ 1,000–2,500).

    Hotel Staff: Tip porters ₨ 200–300 (≈US$1–2) per bag. Give housekeeping ₨ 200–300 per day (≈US$1)
    themiddleagewanderer.com
    . Concierge assistance or special services: ₨ 500–1,000 depending on help provided. If there’s a service charge on room service, additional tip is optional.

    Temples/Religious Sites: Donations (in LKR) are customary. There is no set rate – discreetly drop a few hundred rupees into donation boxes or hand them to a monk/attendant if offering to guide you
    roughguides.com
    . This is appreciated but voluntary.

Common Scams to Watch For

    Tuk-tuk Driver Tricks: The most common scam involves tuk-tuk overcharging. One person may lure you (e.g. hinting at a festival), then a driver “without a meter” appears and demands an inflated fare
    godigit.com
    . They may team up and intimidate you into paying. Always insist on using the meter or agree a fair fare before riding.

    Closed-Attraction Scam: A driver or self-appointed “guide” claims a site (e.g. temple or museum) is closed and offers an alternate tour. They then take you to shops or restaurants where they earn commissions
    godigit.com
    islands.com
    . If someone tells you an attraction is unexpectedly closed, verify the hours yourself or politely decline and walk away.

    Gem and Spice Shop Scams: Tuk-tuk drivers often detour to spice gardens or gem shops for a commission
    godigit.com
    godigit.com
    . You may be pressured to buy overpriced or fake goods. If interested in gems or local products, deal with reputable shops independently.

    Pickpocket Distractions: Be wary in crowded areas. A common trick is the “bird-poop” scam – someone smears a fake stain on you, then a helper cleans it off while stealing valuables
    godigit.com
    . Keep belongings secure, carry money in front pockets, and be extra vigilant in markets and busy streets.

Stay aware: If anything seems suspicious or too good to be true, trust your instincts and refuse politely. Most scams target tourists unprepared for local practices. By knowing these pitfalls, you can avoid them and enjoy a safe trip.
