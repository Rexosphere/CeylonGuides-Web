## **Health and Insurance Guide for Tourists in Sri Lanka: Complete Research 2025**

### **Recommended Vaccinations**

**Routine Vaccinations:**
The CDC and WHO recommend travelers to Sri Lanka be up to date with routine vaccinations including measles-mumps-rubella (MMR), diphtheria-tetanus-pertussis (Tdap), polio, chickenpox, influenza, COVID-19, pneumonia, meningitis, and shingles.

**Travel-Specific Vaccines:**

**Hepatitis A & B:**
Hepatitis A and B vaccinations are recommended for Sri Lanka due to potential food and water contamination. These are particularly important for most travelers visiting the country.

**Typhoid:**
Typhoid vaccination is especially recommended if visiting smaller cities or rural areas, with both oral and injectable typhoid vaccinations available.

**Yellow Fever:**
Yellow fever vaccination is required only if arriving within 6 days after leaving or transiting (more than 12 hours) countries with risk of yellow fever transmission, with exemptions for children under 9 months and passengers not leaving transit areas.

**NEW: Chikungunya Vaccine (2025):**
As of December 13, 2025, the CDC recommends vaccination with a U.S. FDA-approved vaccine for travelers visiting Sri Lanka due to an ongoing Chikungunya outbreak, with vaccination particularly recommended for those visiting outbreak areas. Two vaccines against chikungunya are licensed in Europe: Vimkunya (virus-like particle vaccine) and Ixchiq (live-attenuated vaccine), with the CDC recommending a single-dose vaccine for visitors.

**Special Consideration - Pregnant Travelers:**
Pregnant travelers should reconsider travel to chikungunya-affected areas, particularly if close to delivering, as mothers infected around delivery can pass the virus to their baby, putting newborns at risk for severe illness and poor long-term outcomes.

**Optional Vaccines:**
Depending on your itinerary, personal risk factors, and length of visit, healthcare providers may offer vaccination against dengue, Japanese encephalitis, or rabies.

**Timing:**
Travelers should ideally arrange an appointment with their health professional at least 4-6 weeks before travel to get vaccines or medicines they may need.

**Malaria Status:**
Sri Lanka is officially malaria-free since 2016, so no malaria prophylaxis is required, though bite avoidance is still recommended.

### **Essential Medications & Packing**

**Bringing Personal Medications:**
Take your regular medications with you to Sri Lanka as finding specific brands or alternatives can be tricky—keep medications in their original packaging with a doctor's note or prescription to explain your needs. Typically you can bring enough for personal use for up to 90 days, which aligns with the usual 30-day single-entry visas most tourists receive.

**Controlled Substances:**
Some controlled medicines may need special approval from Sri Lanka's National Medicines Regulatory Authority (NMRA).

**Essential Travel Medicine Kit:**
Based on the original document and research, pack:
- **Antihistamines** for mild allergic reactions to insect bites or food
- **Insect repellent** with DEET or Picaridin (crucial for dengue and chikungunya prevention)
- **Oral Rehydration Salts (ORS)** to recover from dehydration
- **Pain relievers** - Paracetamol is recommended
- **Prescription medications** in original bottles with copies of prescriptions
- **Sunscreen** (SPF 50+ recommended for tropical climate)
- **First-aid supplies** - basic bandages, antiseptic, etc.
- **Motion sickness medication** if needed

**Important Medication Warnings:**
If you have fever, avoid taking medications containing acetylsalicylic acid (e.g., Aspirin) as this can increase the risk of bleeding during dengue or chikungunya infection—however, don't stop taking it if already part of regular treatment for an underlying condition. For dengue/chikungunya, use paracetamol products and avoid other pain-relieving drugs like Ibuprofen, Diclofenac Sodium, or Mefenamic acid.

### **Common Local Health Concerns**

**🦟 Dengue Fever (High Risk - Year-Round):**

**Current Situation:**
Dengue fever cases are on the rise in Sri Lanka—more than 2,000 cases were reported in the first 2 weeks of May 2025 and over 20,000 cases year to date, with almost 50% of notifications coming from Western Province. Sri Lanka reported 24,815 dengue cases and 9 deaths in 2024, with more than 45,000 cases notified.

**Transmission & Risk:**
Dengue is transmitted from person to person via mosquito bite, with the Aedes mosquito most active early in the morning and late afternoon. High risk exists throughout the country, including in deluxe accommodations.

**Seasonal Patterns:**
Outbreaks of dengue are not uncommon in the months following peak wet season, with wettest months generally October to December (regional variation), and two annual peaks in association with monsoon rains.

**Prevention:**
There is no pill or vaccine to protect you from dengue and no full-proof remedy to cure you from it—prevention through mosquito bite protection is essential. The original document correctly recommends using repellent and covering up.

**🦟 Chikungunya Outbreak (High Risk - 2025):**

**Current Alert:**
There is an outbreak of chikungunya in Sri Lanka as of December 2025, with mosquitoes spreading the virus and the CDC issuing a Level 2 - Practice Enhanced Precautions Travel Health Notice. Two cases of Chikungunya have been reported in German travelers returning from Sri Lanka after staying there in January and February 2025.

**Prevention:**
Protect yourself by preventing mosquito bites using insect repellent, wearing long-sleeved shirts and pants, and staying in places with air conditioning or screens on windows and doors.

**☀️ Sun & Heat (High Risk):**

**Climate Considerations:**
Sri Lanka is tropical and much closer to the equator, so naturally the climate is warmer and humid—tourists will feel a lot of heat and sweat more. Sri Lanka is a hot tropical country where you should drink plenty of water to avoid dehydration when touring, and wear sunscreen at beaches to avoid heatstroke.

**Prevention:**
Stay hydrated with water or electrolyte drinks, rest in shade during hottest hours (10 am-4 pm), apply broad-spectrum sunscreen and reapply regularly, wear hats and sunglasses.

**🐛 Leeches (Low-Medium Risk):**
The original document correctly notes leeches are common in rainforest areas. You should wear leech socks when hiking to protect against leech bites in jungle and highland areas.

**Other Health Risks:**

**Leptospirosis:**
Leptospirosis, a bacterial infection that can be spread in fresh water, is found in Sri Lanka—avoid swimming in fresh, unchlorinated water such as lakes, ponds, or rivers.

**Altitude Sickness:**
There is a risk of altitude illness when traveling to destinations of 2,500 metres (8,200 feet) or higher, with important risk factors being altitude gained, rate of ascent and sleeping altitude.

### **Food and Water Safety**

**💧 Tap Water - NOT SAFE:**

**Official Guidance:**
The tap water in Sri Lanka is not considered safe to drink—bottled or filtered water is recommended, as Sri Lanka's tropical climate and inconsistent treatment standards increase the likelihood of microbial contamination.

**Why It's Unsafe:**
Around 80% of rural households rely on groundwater mainly accessed through hand pumps, but in areas like the Kelani River basin, water is often polluted by household waste, agricultural runoff, and industrial discharge. Naturally occurring contaminants like fluoride and heavy metals also affect groundwater, leading to health issues such as fluorosis and chronic kidney disease especially in dry zones.

**Regional Variation:**
Although water distributed in major cities such as Colombo, Kandy or Galle is treated by local authorities, water quality can vary considerably depending on infrastructure and local conditions, with older pipes, leaks and occasional contamination making water unfit for consumption.

**🍼 Bottled Water - Recommended but Check Quality:**
While some locals may drink tap water, it is generally recommended that tourists drink bottled water or water that has been boiled or treated to avoid potential stomach upsets or waterborne illnesses.

**Quality Concerns:**
Some bottled water brands in Colombo are unsuitable for drinking as they exceed permitted levels for one or more prescribed parameters, with certain brands available without SLS (Sri Lanka Standards) certification. Before buying, always check that the bottle cap is properly sealed to avoid unpleasant surprises.

**Practical Water Tips:**
- It's best to use bottled or filtered water when brushing your teeth, since even a small amount of tap water can carry harmful bacteria
- When ordering drinks in restaurants or bars, be careful with ice cubes as they may be made from unpurified tap water—choose bottled drinks or ask whether ice cubes are made from drinking water
- It's safer to rinse produce with bottled or filtered water, especially if you plan to eat it raw, and always wash fruit and vegetables with bottled or purified water

**🍽️ Food Safety:**

**Street Food:**
Food hygiene at restaurants is reasonable but varies depending on where you eat—it's generally safe to try street food as long as it's served hot. Eating street food in Sri Lanka can be a delightful experience when done safely.

**Best Practices:**
The original document's recommendations remain accurate:
- Avoid ice unless from a safe source (high-end hotels)
- Drink bottled water only—check the seal
- Eat cooked food hot in front of you
- Be cautious with raw foods like salads and fruit washed in tap water
- Avoid undercooked meat or unpasteurized dairy

**Why You Still Need It:**
It is highly recommended to buy travel medical insurance—the price of the policy is small in exchange for ensuring a stress-free travel experience, and it provides comprehensive financial protection against hospitalization expenses from accident or illness.
